Contamination-Resilient CRT Design Engine

This console runs the manuscript's pipeline on its frozen public geography: 57 Malaria Atlas Project survey sites in Uganda. Panels marked Live recompute from raw site data in your browser (a JavaScript port of the deposited R code; how closely it agrees with the deposited run is stated in the footer). Panels marked Deposited display Monte-Carlo results from the archived R runs. The trial is simulated. The geography, the survey counts and the connectivity are real; the arms, the intervention and its effect are not. As in the paper's main tables, every figure here assumes no between-site variance beyond binomial sampling; the paper's Appendix D adds between-site and between-cluster heterogeneity (intracluster correlation 0.02 to 0.10), under which a binomial analysis is too narrow even without contamination and the design's precision gains shrink.

3 · Operating characteristics: live Monte Carlo in your browser Live

The full data-generating model is ported to JavaScript: latent log-odds ηi = (1−c·si)·η₀ + c·si·(gravity-weighted cross-arm neighbor mean), with saturating boundary exposure si = 1−exp(−Ei/κi); outcomes y ~ Binomial(N, expit η). True effect δ = ln(0.5) ≈ −0.693 (treatment halves infection odds). Pick a design pool (built live in Panel 1), an estimator, and contamination strength c, then watch bias, coverage and RMSE accumulate replicate by replicate with Monte-Carlo standard errors. CC is the contamination-only baseline: it ranks on leakage alone with no balance constraint, so compare its bias against CRR's to see what the balance criterion buys (and check Panel 1's table for what it costs in covariate imbalance).

5 · Generalization testbeds: two more real geographies LiveDeposited

The same estimand, two-criterion balance-plus-leakage allocation and three estimators as Panels 1 and 3 (not the ten-metric suite or the design-selection engine of Panel 2), exercised on structurally different systems: the 2017–18 Yemen cholera outbreak by governorate (waterborne disease, human-mobility spread, symmetric gravity kernel) and early-2020 COVID-19 across Louisiana's parishes, a directional stress test over real reported incidence (cumulative confirmed cases), not a model of COVID transmission. In both, geography/population/baseline incidence are real; assignment and effect are simulated (hypothetical per-unit survey N = 800). The COVID testbed uses the manuscript's directed production-attraction flux Ki→j = SiRj/d², so it can compare a direction-blind design (CRR_sym) against a direction-aware one (CRR_dir). Each testbed also carries the CC contamination-only baseline (top 40 by symmetric leakage alone, no balance constraint), from PROPOSED_cc_baseline_cholera.R / PROPOSED_cc_baseline_covid.R, with its deposited diagnostics and OC rows embedded verbatim.

What this console does not show

The design reduces cross-arm exposure and the measured residual bias. It does not deliver freedom from SUTVA violation. The design does not restore nominal coverage for the naive or the boundary-censored estimator. All figures assume no between-site variance (paper, Appendix D). The composite ranking is a selection statistic, not a test; no p-value is reported for it. The spillover-adjusted estimator needs the mobility kernel, and its good behavior does not survive a kernel the analysis does not assume. The Type-I columns come from model-based tests; randomization inference is recommended for the primary test and is not evaluated in the manuscript. In the Panel 5 testbeds the survey counts are a hypothetical N = 800 per unit, not real counts.

The live naive cell sits at the top of the Table 15 range, so this draw is near the least favorable of the eight.
