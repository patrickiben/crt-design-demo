# Reading companion to the contamination-resilient cluster-randomized-trial design paper
## How to read this companion
This companion walks the manuscript in reading order. Each entry names the passage it covers, says what that passage is doing, restates it in ordinary language, labels how strong the evidence is, warns about the conclusion a reader is most likely to reach by mistake, and gives one thing to check yourself. It is written for a numerate reader who has taken one course in statistics and has never designed a cluster-randomized trial.
File paths that begin supporting/ refer to the OSF deposit (DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/XPA4S): CSV files are in its outputs/ folder, R scripts in its code/ folder, and CCR_MAP_directed_3D_elevation.html at its top level.
These six statements hold across the whole companion and the whole paper.
- **NC1.** The design reduces cross-arm exposure and the measured residual bias. It does not deliver freedom from SUTVA violation.
- **NC2.** The trial is simulated. The geography, the survey counts and the connectivity are real; the arms, the intervention and its effect are not.
- **NC3.** The design does not restore nominal coverage for the naive or the boundary-censored estimator.
- **NC4.** The composite ranking is a selection statistic, not a test. No p-value is reported for it.
- **NC5.** The spillover-adjusted estimator needs the mobility kernel. Its good behavior does not survive a kernel the analysis does not assume.
- **NC6.** Randomization inference is recommended for the primary test and is not evaluated in this paper.

## Front matter

### E1. The headline coverage numbers: a range, not a single win

**Where we are:** Manuscript lines 57-59. Abstract, the sentence reporting boundary-censored coverage of 0.26 to 0.29 under simple randomization against 0.28 to 0.48 under the two-criterion contamination-resilient design, allocating individual sites, across eight candidate pools (nominal coverage is 0.95), together with the later abstract sentence saying these figures assume no between-site variance.

**What the passage is doing:** Reports the key inference-level result as a same-estimator comparison and as a range over independent candidate pools, so the reader sees design variability next to the gain, and concedes that, at moderate contamination, coverage is not restored by any naive or boundary-censored analysis.

**In ordinary language:** Coverage is the share of trials in which a 95 percent confidence interval holds the true effect. A confidence interval is a range of plausible values. The target here is 0.95. The abstract compares like with like. It uses one estimator, the boundary-censored one, under two designs. Boundary-censored means we drop the 30 percent of sites most exposed to spillover before we do the math. Spillover is infection risk that crosses a site boundary. Contamination is infection risk mixing across the border between the two arms, that is, the treated group and the comparison group. All of these numbers are for moderate contamination. Under simple randomization, coverage is 0.26 to 0.29. Under the contamination-resilient design, the two-criterion design built to limit spillover, it is 0.28 to 0.48. These numbers come from allocating single sites, not whole clusters. The range comes from drawing the pool of candidate allocations eight times. So the design helps. Some draws show a big gain and some show almost none. Even the best case, 0.48, is far below 0.95. The design does not bring coverage back to the target.

**Evidence status:** MODELED_LOCKED. The numbers come from the deposited simulation engines at fixed seeds, not from a field trial.

**Do not accidentally conclude:** Do not read 0.48 as the design's typical performance; it is the top of the range and the single realization shown in Table 1. Do not conclude the design restores valid inference; when individual sites are allocated, no naive or boundary-censored analysis reaches nominal coverage at moderate contamination. Do not read these figures as covering real between-site variation either: they assume none. The appendix adds it as an intracluster correlation, a measure of how much sites differ from one another beyond chance. Once it is present, the binomial analyses fail even without contamination. The analysis of each site, the unit assigned to an arm when sites are allocated, stays calibrated at 0.02 to 0.10. When whole clusters are allocated, at 0.02 to 0.05, the analysis of whole clusters comes close, with a false-alarm rate of up to 0.084. Under contamination, when single sites are allocated, the boundary-censored coverage gain persists. The gain in RMSE, a measure of typical error size, is smaller once the intracluster correlation reaches 0.05. These are fixed-seed simulation outputs laid over real geography, not field results.

**Try it:** Compute the width of each range, 0.29 minus 0.26 and 0.48 minus 0.28, and say which design's performance depends more on the luck of the candidate draw.

### E2. What the public-data edition is and is not

**Where we are:** Manuscript lines 57-59. Abstract, the closing sentences declaring that assignments and effects are simulated while the geography and survey counts are real, and naming the Yemen cholera and Louisiana COVID-19 corroborating geographies.

**What the passage is doing:** Declares the evidential architecture of this edition: every analysis runs on openly available, real measured geography and baseline disease rates, while the trial, the intervention, and its effect are simulated on top, and no conducted trial is being reported.

**In ordinary language:** Every data set in this edition is open and free to download. The Uganda malaria survey points are real, dated public records. So are the Yemen cholera counts and the Louisiana COVID counts. The map and the baseline disease rates are measured facts. Population is handled differently in each testbed. In Uganda the number of people examined at each survey site stands in for population. In Yemen the population is back-calculated from case counts and attack rates, a relative stand-in rather than a census. Only the Louisiana parishes use real populations, U.S. Census Bureau 2019 estimates. The trial is not real. The paper splits the sites into two arms, that is, two treatment groups. It then simulates the intervention and its effect on top of this real geography. So any trial performance number, such as bias or coverage, comes from simulation. Bias is how far off an estimate sits on average. Coverage is how often a confidence interval holds the true value. The abstract states that the assignments and effects are simulated. It states that the geography and survey counts are real.

**Evidence status:** SCOPE. The passage bounds what the results cover; it states a limit rather than a finding.

**Do not accidentally conclude:** Do not conclude the operating characteristics are field results from real trials; equally, do not dismiss the geography as toy data. The correct split for the malaria testbed: coordinates, survey denominators, and parasite rates are measured and dated, while everything downstream of the simulated intervention is modeled. In the Yemen and Louisiana testbeds the outcome's denominator is simulated as well, a hypothetical survey of 800 per governorate or parish. Also, reported case counts reflect testing intensity as well as transmission, a caveat the manuscript carries through its later descriptive readings.

**Try it:** Make a two-column list for the malaria testbed: real (coordinates, parasite rates, survey denominators) versus simulated (arm assignment, intervention, treatment effect). Check your list against the abstract sentence that draws exactly this line.

## Introduction

### E4. Contamination and bias toward the null

**Where we are:** Manuscript lines 67-67. Introduction, the opening paragraph, where bidirectional spillover across cluster boundaries is described as biasing treatment effect estimates toward the null.

**What the passage is doing:** Sets up the core problem the paper addresses: in spatially connected settings, treatment effects leak across cluster boundaries in both directions, and this leakage systematically shrinks the estimated effect toward zero rather than merely adding noise.

**In ordinary language:** A cluster randomized trial assigns whole communities, such as villages, to treatment or control. It does not assign single people. Contamination means infection risk mixes across community borders between the trial's arms. Mosquitoes move and people travel. So control villages near treated villages get some protection. Treated villages also keep taking in parasites from untreated neighbors. Both leaks make the two arms, treated and control, look more alike. The effect we measure then shrinks toward zero. That shift is called bias toward the null. The intervention itself is not weaker. Only the comparison between the two arms is blurred.

**Evidence status:** INTERPRETIVE. This is the author's reading of the evidence, not a separate measurement.

**Do not accidentally conclude:** Do not conclude that contamination is merely a power problem that a larger sample would fix, or that a null result from a contaminated trial proves the intervention failed. The passage argues the damage is systematic bias, not random noise, so more data alone converges to the wrong answer. Also do not conclude bias toward the null is safely conservative. It can bury a genuinely effective intervention, and the paper's simulations show the opposite error too: on the malaria geography at moderate contamination under simple randomization, the naive test's Type-I error, its rate of declaring an effect when the treatment effect is zero, averages 0.730 over the eight candidate pools (Limitation 4), because at zero treatment effect the simulated contamination still mixes baseline infection risk across the two arms according to the assignment, and the naive analysis leaves that mixing out. That figure measures the omitted mixing of baseline risk, not spillover of the treatment effect, and it assumes no variation between sites beyond binomial sampling.

**Try it:** List the two leak directions named in the Introduction's opening paragraph, positive spillover into control villages and efficacy leakage into intervention villages. For each, write one line showing why it moves the between-arm difference toward zero.

### E5. SUTVA, partial interference, and the estimand

**Where we are:** Manuscript lines 67-71. Introduction, the opening paragraph stating SUTVA together with the later paragraph that adopts the exposure-mapping machinery of the interference literature (not partial interference, which the paper sets aside in the estimand subsection) to define the boundary-insulated effect as the target estimand.

**What the passage is doing:** Defines the assumption that contamination violates (SUTVA), the formulation the paper actually works under (an exposure-mapping summary of spatial interference, explicitly not partial interference), and the causal quantity the design is built to target (the boundary-insulated effect).

**In ordinary language:** SUTVA is the Stable Unit Treatment Value Assumption. Its no-interference part says each community's outcome depends only on its own treatment. It must never depend on what its neighbors get. Infectious disease breaks this rule, because infections cross borders. So the paper does not assume that spillover stops at some group boundary. Instead, it scores each site's exposure to spillover with an assumed mobility model. Each site gets a cross-arm exposure score. That score adds up the pull from every site in the other arm. Each pull is weighted by how many people were surveyed at each site and by how close the two sites are. The paper states its design does not rely on partial interference, the assumption that spillover happens within defined groups of clusters but never between them. In any allocation the trial can use, both arms hold sites, so no site reaches zero exposure. Some sites simply have far less of it than others. An estimand is the exact quantity a study sets out to estimate. The paper's estimand is the boundary-insulated effect. That is the treatment effect a site would show if it had zero cross-arm exposure. Zero exposure happens only when every other site shares its arm. So the target compares every site treated with every site untreated. It is a total effect. It is not a direct effect with the neighbors held fixed. No site actually has zero exposure. So this is a limit the paper approaches among its least-exposed sites, the core zone. It is not the effect in a group of fully shielded sites.

**Evidence status:** DEFINITION. The passage fixes what a term or a quantity means; nothing here is an empirical result.

**Do not accidentally conclude:** Do not conclude that the framework restores SUTVA or makes interference disappear. It instead defines an estimand that remains meaningful under interference and then designs the trial to reduce cross-arm exposure. Later sections state the estimand is approximately recovered among core-zone units: bias is reduced, not eliminated.

**Try it:** Write the potential outcome of one village as a function of its own assignment and its neighbors' assignments. Mark which dependence terms SUTVA forbids. Then say which of those the paper's exposure score keeps, since no allocation the trial can use gives any site zero cross-arm exposure.

### E6. Balance is not contamination control

**Where we are:** Manuscript lines 69-71. Introduction, the paragraph criticizing geographic buffering and Euclidean distance-based partitioning together with the following literature paragraph, where covariate balance is said not to control contamination bias.

**What the passage is doing:** States the paper's central negative claim: the field-standard balance remedy, covariate-constrained randomization, controls baseline balance, but balance and contamination are different failure modes, and controlling one does not control the other. The claim is asserted here and demonstrated later by simulation.

**In ordinary language:** A cluster randomized trial assigns whole groups, such as villages, to two arms. Each such group is a cluster. A covariate is a trait measured before the trial starts, such as height above sea level or the disease rate at the start. Balance means the two arms start out alike on their covariates. The standard tool for this is covariate-constrained randomization. It draws a random split, then throws that split out if the arms differ too much at the start. Balance guards against a bad draw that leaves the starting groups lopsided. Contamination is a different failure. It is infection risk mixing between the arms across their shared borders after the groups are assigned. A design can be balanced and still put every cluster next to a cluster in the other arm. The paper's central negative claim is that balance alone does not control bias from contamination. The claim is stated here. The support is the study of operating characteristics reported later, which shows how each design performs over many simulated trials.

**Evidence status:** MODELED_LOCKED. The numbers come from the deposited simulation engines at fixed seeds, not from a field trial.

**Do not accidentally conclude:** Do not conclude that balance is useless or can be discarded. Balance still guards against baseline confounding, and it is one of the two criteria of the two-criterion design (CRR) that the operating-characteristics study uses. The manuscript later shows the trade-off cuts both ways across geographies, so that two-criterion design is a robustness compromise, not a claim that contamination control always dominates balance. And do not conclude that the framework's own contamination scores follow the environmental corridors, such as waterways and elevation, that the passage faults distance-based designs for missing: they are built from distances between sites, baseline prevalence and survey size alone, and the environmental covariates enter its balance, topology and free-energy terms, not its contamination scores.

**Try it:** Sketch a checkerboard assignment of villages to two arms. Balance on almost any covariate can be excellent, yet every village borders the opposite arm. State which failure mode this design addresses and which it maximizes.

### E7. Positioning among prior work and the anchoring limit

**Where we are:** Manuscript lines 71-71. Introduction, the literature-synthesis paragraph that runs through the Leung boundary-estimator lineage and the Watson et al. identification result and closes with the spatial-analysis contamination models, which it presents as analysis-side alternatives to the gravity-flux model, neither nesting the other.

**What the passage is doing:** Locates the framework at the junction of three literatures and concedes a proved identification limit: the cited Watson et al. result is a theorem in that work, and the paper positions its own core-zone estimator as an anchored estimator subject to that limit.

**In ordinary language:** This passage places the framework in three lines of past work. Then it names a hard limit. Causal inference under interference gives the estimand, which is the exact thing the design aims to measure. Interference means that a village's result can depend on how other villages were treated. Design-based work gives buffering, which leaves unused space between the arms. It also gives randomization that is constrained on covariates. Mobility research gives the gravity model, which says flow between two places grows with their sizes and shrinks with the distance between them. The design keeps that predicted flow between the arms low. Watson and colleagues prove a limit. It holds for cluster randomization with a fixed exposure set size. A spillover kernel is the curve for how an effect leaks with distance. The data identify the shape of that curve, but not its level. So every direct-effect estimator rests on an anchoring assumption. That is a chosen reference point that fixes the level. The paper's boundary-censored core-zone estimator drops the 30 percent of sites with the highest cross-arm exposure and treats the rest as approximately unexposed. The paper's target is a total effect, not a direct effect. Still, this estimator fixes the spillover level by assumption, so it is anchored too. The claim here is that the design cuts exposure. It does not pin down the effect free of assumptions.

**Evidence status:** INTERPRETIVE. This is the author's reading of the evidence, not a separate measurement.

**Do not accidentally conclude:** Do not conclude that the boundary-censored estimator identifies the target effect free of assumptions, or that this paper escapes the shape-versus-level limit. It does not claim to. What stays anchored is the level: the paper fixes it by treating the lowest-exposure sites as approximately unexposed, and the residual bias its contamination sweep measures is the footprint of the level term that censoring cannot remove. The shape term, error from misspecification of the kernel itself, is probed directly, in the kernel-misspecification sensitivity analysis, where the contamination is generated by kernels the design and the analysis do not assume, and where the spillover-adjusted estimator loses its nominal coverage. Also do not read the paragraph as claiming novelty over Leung's boundary exclusion; the paper presents its estimator as the analysis-side analogue of that device. And do not read the finding that fewer, larger clusters reduce perimeter contamination as free: it is the bias side of a trade-off whose variance side the main simulations do not charge, and Appendix D, which does charge it, finds the coarser pools giving up power.

**Try it:** Pose this question to any effect estimate in the paper: what assumption pins the spillover level here? For the core-zone estimator, find the paper's answer and check it against the shape-versus-level distinction stated in this paragraph.

### E71. The framework's objective and its three components

**Where we are:** Manuscript lines 73-73. Introduction, the short paragraph that closes the framing material and states in one sentence what the optimization framework is and what it optimizes, just before the Interaction-Aware Feature Space subsection.

**What the passage is doing:** This is the thesis sentence of the Introduction. It turns the preceding catalog of risks into a single stated objective, baseline covariate exchangeability set against proxy measures of topological fragmentation and cross-arm contamination exposure, and it names the machinery that supplies that objective: graph-theoretic spatial clustering, Persistent Homology from Topological Data Analysis, and an ensemble decision engine, through which the framework jointly considers operational geometry, spatial topology, and statistical identity. It is the Introduction's statement of what the whole apparatus is for, ahead of the Methods section that groups the ten metrics into three pillars, and it attaches a limited claim to it: robustness to measured spatial and mobility-related contamination routes rather than their removal.

**In ordinary language:** This sentence says what the tool is. The tool searches many ways to split the study sites into two arms. A site is one village or survey point that can be assigned. An arm is one of the two groups in a trial. The tool gives each split a score, and the score has three parts. The first part is balance on covariates. A covariate is a trait measured before the trial starts, such as height above sea level. Balanced arms start out alike on those traits. The older method here is constrained randomization. It draws a split at random, then throws it out when the arms differ too much at the start. This framework keeps that goal and adds two more parts. The second part is shape. The tool uses clustering, which gathers nearby sites into groups. It also uses persistent homology, a way to count the holes and gaps in a pattern of points. That method comes from topological data analysis, the study of shape in data. The third part is exposure to contamination. Contamination is infection risk that mixes across the border between the two arms. The tool cannot watch leakage happen. It uses stand-ins, or proxies, built from distance and from travel between places. An ensemble engine then blends the three parts into one ranking. Ensemble here means the parts are pooled by set rules rather than by taste. The promise made is careful. The chosen split should hold up better against the leak routes the data can see.

**Evidence status:** DEFINITION. The passage fixes what a term or a quantity means; nothing here is an empirical result.

**Do not accidentally conclude:** Do not read the words optimization and maximize as a guarantee about the design that comes out. The framework ranks candidate configurations on a composite score and keeps a high-ranking one; that composite is a selection statistic, not a test, so a high score is not evidence that the arms are exchangeable or that spillover has been designed away. Take seriously what the sentence itself concedes: the topology and contamination terms are proxy measures of fragmentation and exposure, not observed leakage, and the robustness claimed reaches only measured spatial and mobility-related sources. The design reduces cross-arm exposure and residual bias without delivering freedom from SUTVA violation; it does not restore nominal coverage for the naive or boundary-censored estimator; and the spillover-adjusted estimator paired with it still depends on the mobility kernel, which the paper tests at site-level allocation only. Randomization-based inference is recommended in this paper but not evaluated in it.

**Try it:** Open supporting/PROPOSED_design_selection_MAP.csv and read only its header row. Try sorting the ten metric columns into the three pillars of the manuscript's 10-Metric Diagnostic Suite: d_balance, d_wasser and d_density_symmetry belong to Pillar 1, Statistical Interchangeability, the statistical identity axis this sentence names; d_euler, d_persistence_gap, d_vfe and d_spatial_mixing belong to Pillar 2, Topological and Spatial Cohesion, which carries both the spatial topology and the operational geometry of the sentence, since the paper reports the Euler Characteristic, Persistence Gap and free-energy terms as operational-feasibility diagnostics; and d_contam_auc, d_frontier_auc_exposure and d_mobility_auc_leakage belong to Pillar 3, Contamination Resilience. The composite_score column is the ensemble output the sentence promises. Check your sorting against the metrics section of the manuscript, which also notes that d_spatial_mixing, though placed in Pillar 2, is a second functional of the same cross-arm risk-asymmetry profile as the Contamination AUC, so the pillars group the metrics without partitioning what each one measures.

### E9. Engineered eco-epidemiological interaction covariates

**Where we are:** Manuscript lines 84-91. Study Data and Context, the paragraph defining the baseline covariate matrix, the numbered list of the two eco-epidemiological interaction terms, and the sentence after it saying where those terms enter.

**What the passage is doing:** Defines the baseline covariate matrix and justifies adding two spatial interaction terms (elevation times water proximity, latitude times water proximity), so that candidate arms are compared on compound environmental risk profiles, through the Wasserstein Distance and the Density Symmetry metric, rather than only on marginal averages. The Population Balance metric uses the four marginal covariates alone.

**In ordinary language:** A covariate is a background variable measured before the trial, such as elevation. The design tries to balance the two arms on these variables. The author adds two product terms. One multiplies elevation by a water-proximity term. The other multiplies standardized latitude by that same term. The motivating idea is that mosquito breeding risk spikes where low ground sits next to water. In the public build, though, there is no hydrology layer at all. Water proximity is a rank of elevation that scores how low-lying a site is, from 0 to 1. So the first product is a nonlinear function of elevation alone, and the second is standardized latitude times that elevation rank. Both stand in for what a real trial would compute from actual water data. Both products enter the Wasserstein Distance, a score that compares the whole spread of the arms' covariates, as two extra features. The first product also sets the weight on each site's area in the Density Symmetry score. The Population Balance score leaves both out. So candidate arms get compared on combinations, not just on each variable's separate average.

**Evidence status:** DEFINITION. The passage fixes what a term or a quantity means; nothing here is an empirical result.

**Do not accidentally conclude:** Do not conclude that the paper estimated or verified these ecological synergy effects on the Uganda data. The interaction terms are design inputs motivated by vector-ecology reasoning. Balancing on them lowers the chance that sites high on these computed combinations end up concentrated in one arm, which bounds one route to confounding if the combinations matter. It does not guarantee equality between the arms, and it does not demonstrate that the combinations matter.

**Try it:** Ask what balancing on an interaction costs if the interaction is actually pure noise. Reason it through: some balancing effort is spent on an irrelevant column, but no bias is introduced, which is why such terms function as cheap insurance.

### E10. Two distance rulers: standardized coordinates for search, great-circle distance for scoring

**Where we are:** Manuscript lines 93-93. Study Data and Context, the closing paragraph on the dual-resolution distance architecture and the standardized clustering space.

**What the passage is doing:** Describes the two distance spaces: great-circle (haversine) distances on a sphere form the matrix D_geo, which anchors the evaluation suite, with every metric scored on D_geo, on UTM-projected areas or on a kilometer point cloud, while candidate partitions are generated on longitude and latitude standardized to unit variance, which is disclosed as a deliberate anisotropic rescaling rather than an approximation to a metric.

**In ordinary language:** The framework uses two distance rulers. To score a plan, it uses great-circle distance in kilometers, the shortest path over a sphere. On this geography, that distance stays within 0.6 percent of the true ellipsoid distance. The fast search step uses a different ruler. It centers longitude and latitude, then divides each by its spread across the 57 sites; statisticians call that standardizing. Latitude varies less than longitude here. So one degree of latitude counts 1.33 times one degree of longitude in the search space. That favors clusters stretched east to west. The rescaling only changes which groupings get proposed. Every metric is scored in kilometers, so no score inherits it.

**Evidence status:** ANALYTIC. The statement follows from the definitions and the algebra, with no data involved.

**Do not accidentally conclude:** Do not conclude the search space is a flat, undistorted map; the paper now says plainly that it is stretched by a factor of 1.33 along latitude. Do not conclude that stretch reaches any result: scoring runs on the kilometer matrix, projected areas or the kilometer point cloud. And do not read the distances as WGS 84 geodesics; they are spherical haversine distances, which the paper states.

**Try it:** Take two sites one degree apart in longitude and two sites one degree apart in latitude. Compute their distances in the standardized search space using the two standard deviations quoted in the passage, 1.035 and 0.780 degrees, and confirm the 1.33 ratio.

## Methods

### E54. The workflow figure: the pipeline before its parts

**Where we are:** Manuscript lines 98-130. Methods, the opening sentence of the section together with the workflow figure it sends the reader to before any single stage is defined.

**What the passage is doing:** The sentence hands the reader the architecture of the whole procedure in one figure before the subsections define each stage. The figure fixes the order of the steps, from the public survey sites through candidate cluster generation, metric evaluation, score standardization, ensemble weighting and final design selection, and shows the random-allocation null baseline entering as a side branch rather than as the last step.

**In ordinary language:** A workflow figure is a map of the steps a method takes, in order. This one starts with the real survey sites. It ends with a single featured design, the top-ranked member of an elite pool of the 40 highest-scoring candidates. ICC measures how alike two people in the same place are, beyond what the measured baseline explains. The paper tries ICC values of 0, 0.02, 0.05 and 0.10 for single sites, and up to 0.05 for whole clusters. Then it analyzes each simulated trial in several ways. With no contamination at all, the paper's main analysis gives intervals that are far too narrow once ICC is above zero. That main analysis is a binomial model that treats every examined person as independent. At site allocation its coverage falls to between 0.200 and 0.622. An analysis at the level of the site, which treats each site as one data point, stays on target, covering 0.938 to 0.961. The two-stage cluster-level analysis first averages within each cluster. It comes close, covering 0.928 to 0.959, with a false-positive rate a little above its target. The mixed-model alternative, called a GLMM, is too liberal, so the paper does not use its intervals.

**Evidence status:** MODELED_LOCKED. The numbers come from the deposited simulation engines at fixed seeds, not from a field trial.

**Do not accidentally conclude:** Do not read the ICC values as estimates for any real trial; they are a grid chosen to show sensitivity, and the paper does not say which value applies. Do not read 'stays on target' as proof that the site-level analysis is right under contamination; this paragraph checks the case with no contamination. And do not read the binomial model's failure here as a coding error: at ICC 0 it is the true model, and it fails only because the added variation is real in this simulation.
